# Crystal Lattices and First Brillouin Zones

A Bravais lattice is generated by integer combinations of three primitive translations. Its
reciprocal lattice organizes the wavevectors that are equivalent in a periodic crystal. The first
Brillouin zone is the set of reciprocal-space points closer to the origin than to any other
reciprocal-lattice point.

## Reciprocal lattice and the first Brillouin zone

For primitive vectors $\mathbf a_i$, the reciprocal basis $\mathbf b_j$ is defined by

$$
\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf b_j=2\pi\delta_{ij}.
$$

Every nonzero reciprocal vector $\mathbf G$ supplies one perpendicular-bisector inequality,

$$
\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf G\leq\frac{|\mathbf G|^2}{2}.
$$

Their common intersection is the Wigner–Seitz cell of the reciprocal lattice: the first Brillouin
zone. If $V_{\mathrm{cell}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{BZ}}$ are the primitive-cell volumes in real and
reciprocal space, then

$$
V_{\mathrm{cell}}V_{\mathrm{BZ}}=(2\pi)^3.
$$

## Things to try

1. Compare simple cubic, body-centered cubic, and face-centered cubic lattices. Track how the
   reciprocal lattice exchanges BCC and FCC.
2. Hide the reciprocal-lattice points, then restore them and identify which nearby points define
   the faces of the zone.
3. Reduce the zone opacity and rotate both views to compare the real-space primitive basis with
   the reciprocal-space polyhedron.

## What to notice

- A simple-cubic lattice has a cubic first Brillouin zone.
- The BCC zone is a rhombic dodecahedron because its reciprocal lattice is FCC; the FCC zone is a
  truncated octahedron because its reciprocal lattice is BCC.
- The simple-hexagonal lattice gives a hexagonal prism. Changing $c/a$ changes its aspect ratio,
  but not this combinatorial type away from degeneracies.

## Conventions and limitations

Lengths are dimensionless: the cubic conventional-cell edge is $1$, while the hexagonal example
uses $a=1$ and $c/a=1.6$. The displayed cells are ideal Bravais lattices without an atomic basis.
The zone is constructed numerically from reciprocal points in a finite neighboring shell; that
shell is sufficient for all four lattices shown.
