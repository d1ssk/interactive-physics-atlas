# Crystal Lattices and First Brillouin Zones

A three-dimensional Bravais lattice is generated by integer combinations of three primitive
translation vectors. For waves in a crystal, the wavevectors $\mathbf k$ and $\mathbf k+\mathbf G$
are closely related through the reciprocal-lattice vector $\mathbf G$. The reciprocal lattice
expresses the periodicity of the crystal in wavevector space.

The first Brillouin zone is defined as the region closest to the origin of the reciprocal lattice.
It is the Wigner–Seitz cell of the reciprocal lattice and serves as the fundamental region for
organizing wavevectors in a crystal.

## From the reciprocal lattice to the first Brillouin zone

For real-space primitive vectors $\mathbf a_i$, the reciprocal primitive vectors $\mathbf b_j$ are
defined by

$$
\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf b_j=2\pi\delta_{ij}
$$

A reciprocal-lattice vector can then be written as

$$
\mathbf G=n_1\mathbf b_1+n_2\mathbf b_2+n_3\mathbf b_3,
\qquad n_i\in\mathbb Z
$$

The first Brillouin zone is the set of points $\mathbf k$ closer to the reciprocal-lattice origin
than to any other reciprocal-lattice point. For every nonzero reciprocal vector $\mathbf G$, the
plane bisecting the segment from the origin to $\mathbf G$ is a candidate boundary. The half-space
on the side containing the origin is

$$
\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf G\leq\frac{|\mathbf G|^2}{2}
$$

The intersection of these half-spaces for every $\mathbf G\neq 0$ is the reciprocal-lattice
Wigner–Seitz cell, or first Brillouin zone.

If $V_{\mathrm{cell}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{BZ}}$ are the primitive-cell volumes in real and reciprocal
space, respectively, then

$$
V_{\mathrm{cell}}V_{\mathrm{BZ}}=(2\pi)^3
$$

This relation expresses the inverse scaling of real and reciprocal space: the larger the real-space
unit cell, the smaller the fundamental region in reciprocal space.

## What the visualization shows

Switch among the simple-cubic, body-centered-cubic (BCC), and face-centered-cubic (FCC) lattices
to compare each real lattice with its reciprocal lattice. The reciprocal of the simple-cubic
lattice is again simple cubic, and its first Brillouin zone is a cube.

BCC and FCC exchange roles under reciprocal-lattice construction. Consequently, the first
Brillouin zone of BCC is the rhombic-dodecahedral Wigner–Seitz cell of an FCC lattice, while the
first Brillouin zone of FCC is the truncated-octahedral Wigner–Seitz cell of a BCC lattice.

Toggle the reciprocal-lattice points to see how the perpendicular-bisector plane associated with
each point defines a face of the first Brillouin zone. Lowering the zone opacity and rotating the
view makes the relation between the reciprocal points and the polyhedron easier to see.

For the simple-hexagonal lattice, the first Brillouin zone is a hexagonal prism. Changing $c/a$
changes the ratio of its height to its width, but the basic hexagonal-prism shape remains as long
as the lattice symmetry is preserved.

## Display conventions

Lengths are dimensionless. The conventional-cell edge is $1$ for the cubic lattices; the
hexagonal lattice uses $a=1$ and $c/a=1.6$.

The examples are Bravais lattices without an atomic basis. The visualization constructs each first
Brillouin zone numerically as a Wigner–Seitz cell from the surrounding reciprocal-lattice points.
