# Two-Dimensional Conformal Transformations

Two-dimensional conformal geometry connects complex analysis, two-dimensional conformal field
theory, and worldsheet geometry in string theory. The same conformal symmetry appears locally as
angle preservation by holomorphic maps, globally as Möbius transformations of the Riemann
sphere, and infinitesimally as the Witt algebra.

## Local conformality and Wirtinger derivatives

Let the complex coordinates of the source and target be $z=x+iy$ and $w=f(z,\bar z)$,
respectively. The first-order change of a real-differentiable map decomposes as

$$
dw=A\,dz+B\,d\bar z,
\qquad
A=\partial_z f,
\qquad
B=\partial_{\bar z}f
$$

The two singular values of this real-linear map are

$$
\sigma_{\max}=|A|+|B|,
\qquad
\sigma_{\min}=\bigl||A|-|B|\bigr|
$$

Thus, in the local linearization, a small circle maps to an ellipse with axis ratio

$$
K=
\frac{|A|+|B|}
{\bigl||A|-|B|\bigr|}
$$

When $|A|=|B|$, the smaller singular value $\sigma_{\min}$ vanishes and the differential
becomes degenerate.

A holomorphic map is orientation-preserving and conformal wherever $B=0$ and $A\ne0$. An
antiholomorphic map has $A=0$ and $B\ne0$, preserving angles while reversing orientation. If
$A$ and $B$ are both nonzero, the two singular values generally differ and the map is not
conformal.

The first panel compares the exact image of a small circle with its first-order linearization.
The two agree increasingly well as the circle shrinks; the visualization uses a finite radius so
that nonlinear effects remain visible.

## Global transformations of the Riemann sphere

Adding a single point at infinity to the complex plane gives the Riemann sphere
$\mathbb{CP}^1$. Every bijective holomorphic self-map of this sphere is a Möbius transformation

$$
z\longmapsto \frac{az+b}{cz+d},
\qquad
ad-bc\ne0
$$

In homogeneous coordinates it is the projective action of the linear transformation

$$
[u:v]\longmapsto[au+bv:cu+dv]
$$

Multiplying the entire matrix by any nonzero complex number leaves the projective
transformation unchanged, so the automorphism group is

$$
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{CP}^1)
\simeq \mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb C)
\simeq \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb C)
$$

Möbius transformations map generalized circles—the circles and lines of the complex plane—to
generalized circles. They also preserve the ordered cross ratio

$$
(z_1,z_2;z_3,z_4)
=
\frac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}
{(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)}
$$

On the sphere, a point where the denominator of the plane-coordinate formula vanishes is not a
global singularity. It is simply mapped to the point at infinity.

## Witt generators

Holomorphic infinitesimal conformal transformations in a local complex coordinate $z$ are
generated by

$$
\ell_m=-z^{m+1}\partial_z,
\qquad
m\in\mathbb Z
$$

and satisfy

$$
[\ell_m,\ell_n]
=
(m-n)\ell_{m+n}
$$

This is the Witt algebra without a central extension.

The vector fields that are holomorphic everywhere on the Riemann sphere are precisely

$$
\ell_{-1},\qquad
\ell_0,\qquad
\ell_1
$$

These generate the global Möbius transformations. The other modes define local conformal vector
fields but have a pole at $z=0$ or $z=\infty$.

For $m\ne0$, the local finite flow is

$$
\Phi_{m,\epsilon}(z)
=
\frac{z}
{\left(1+m\epsilon z^m\right)^{1/m}},
$$

whereas for $m=0$ it is

$$
\Phi_{0,\epsilon}(z)=e^{-\epsilon}z
$$

Whenever a complex power appears, the visualization selects its principal branch. In
particular, the finite flows with $|m|>1$ are not global automorphisms of the Riemann sphere and
must be interpreted as local representations.

## Suggested explorations

1. Compare $f(z)=z^2$, $f(z)=\bar z$, and $f(z)=z+\lambda\bar z$, and relate the two principal
   axes of the small circle to $\sigma_{\max}$ and $\sigma_{\min}$.

2. As $\lambda$ approaches $1$, observe how the smaller singular value approaches zero and the
   differential degenerates to rank one.

3. Switch among the Möbius presets and rotate the Riemann sphere to inspect the fixed points and
   coordinate curves. Their crossing angles remain unchanged by the transformation.

4. Compare the Witt modes $m=-1,0,1$ with $m=\pm2$. Reversing the sign of $\epsilon$ lets you
   distinguish the direction of the finite flow from that of the positive-parameter vector
   field.

## Relation to string theory

After fixing the worldsheet metric to conformal gauge in string theory, local conformal
transformations remain as residual symmetries. On a Euclidean worldsheet they are described by
holomorphic and antiholomorphic transformations.

The Witt algebra discussed here is the classical algebra of holomorphic vector fields. In a
quantum conformal field theory, the Laurent modes $L_m$ of the stress tensor satisfy the
centrally extended Virasoro algebra

$$
[L_m,L_n]
=
(m-n)L_{m+n}
+\frac{c}{12}m(m^2-1)\delta_{m+n,0}
$$

This visualization does not include quantum structures such as fields, correlation functions,
operator product expansions, or the central charge. It focuses on the geometry of conformal
transformations and the classical Witt algebra. Although the display primarily shows one
holomorphic sector, the local panel also includes the $\partial_{\bar z}f$ term to demonstrate
how an antiholomorphic component can destroy conformality.
