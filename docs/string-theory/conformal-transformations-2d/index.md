# Two-Dimensional Conformal Transformations

Two-dimensional conformal geometry connects complex analysis, the symmetry algebra of
two-dimensional conformal field theory, and the geometry of a string worldsheet. The same idea
appears locally as angle preservation, globally as a transformation of the Riemann sphere, and
infinitesimally as the Witt algebra.

## Local conformality and Wirtinger derivatives

Write the coordinates of the source and target planes as $z=x+iy$ and $w=f(z,\bar z)$. The
first-order change of a real-differentiable map can be decomposed as

$$
dw=A\,dz+B\,d\bar z,
\qquad
A=\partial_z f,
\qquad
B=\partial_{\bar z}f.
$$

The two singular values of this real-linear differential are

$$
\sigma_{\max}=|A|+|B|,
\qquad
\sigma_{\min}=\bigl||A|-|B|\bigr|.
$$

Thus a small circle becomes an ellipse with axis ratio

$$
K=\frac{|A|+|B|}{\bigl||A|-|B|\bigr|}.
$$

A holomorphic map is orientation-preserving and conformal wherever $B=0$ and $A\ne0$.
An antiholomorphic map has $A=0$ and $B\ne0$: it preserves unsigned angles but reverses
orientation. A general map with both terms present is not conformal unless one coefficient
vanishes.

The first panel compares the exact image of a small circle with its linearized image. Their
agreement improves as the circle is made smaller; the visualization keeps a visible finite
radius so that nonlinear effects remain discernible.

## Global maps of the Riemann sphere

Adding the point at infinity compactifies the complex plane to the Riemann sphere
$\mathbb{CP}^1$. Every bijective holomorphic self-map of this sphere is a Möbius transformation

$$
z\longmapsto \frac{az+b}{cz+d},
\qquad
ad-bc\ne0.
$$

In homogeneous coordinates it is simply the projective action

$$
[u:v]\longmapsto[au+bv:cu+dv].
$$

Multiplying the matrix by any nonzero complex number leaves the projective map unchanged, so the
group is $\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb C)\cong\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb C)$. Möbius transformations send
generalized circles—circles or lines in the plane chart—to generalized circles and preserve the
ordered cross ratio

$$
(z_1,z_2;z_3,z_4)
=\frac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}{(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)}.
$$

The sphere panel avoids treating a pole of the plane formula as a singular point of the global
map: it is an ordinary point whose image is infinity.

## Witt generators

Holomorphic infinitesimal conformal transformations in a complex chart are generated by

$$
\ell_m=-z^{m+1}\partial_z,
\qquad m\in\mathbb Z,
$$

with Lie bracket

$$
[\ell_m,\ell_n]=(m-n)\ell_{m+n}.
$$

Only $m=-1,0,1$ are everywhere regular on the Riemann sphere. They generate the global Möbius
subgroup. The remaining modes are valid local conformal vector fields but have a pole in one of
the sphere charts. For $m\ne0$, the finite local flow used in the visualization is

$$
\Phi_{m,\epsilon}(z)
=\frac{z}{\left(1+m\epsilon z^m\right)^{1/m}},
$$

where the principal complex-power branch is selected. This branch choice is why the displayed
finite transformations for $|m|>1$ must not be read as global automorphisms.

## Suggested explorations

1. Compare $f(z)=z^2$, $f(z)=\bar z$, and $f(z)=z+\lambda\bar z$. Watch the local circle and
   relate its axes to $\sigma_{\max}$ and $\sigma_{\min}$.

2. Increase $\lambda$ toward $1$. The smaller singular value approaches zero and the local map
   approaches a rank-one, degenerate differential.

3. Rotate the Riemann sphere for each Möbius preset. Identify the fixed points and confirm that
   transformed coordinate curves still cross at the same angles.

4. Compare the Witt modes $m=-1,0,1$ with $m=\pm2$. Reverse the sign of $\epsilon$ and distinguish
   the direction of the finite flow from the positive-parameter vector field.

## Relation to string theory and limitations

For a string worldsheet, conformal transformations act on the two-dimensional coordinates after
fixing the worldsheet metric to a conformal gauge. The visualization shows the classical,
holomorphic, centerless algebra. It does not display fields, correlation functions, the stress
tensor, operator product expansions, or the quantum central extension

$$
[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}
+\frac{c}{12}m(m^2-1)\delta_{m+n,0}.
$$

The holomorphic and antiholomorphic sectors of a full two-dimensional conformal field theory are
independent before reality and boundary conditions are imposed. This visualization concentrates
on one holomorphic sector, except in the local panel where the $\partial_{\bar z}f$ term is shown
explicitly to diagnose loss of conformality.
